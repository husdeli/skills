# Social Writing

## Purpose and reader

Readers scroll fast and decide within one line whether to stop. Nobody asked for this post, nobody
owes it attention, and the next thing in the feed is always one thumb away.

- **The reader is mid-scroll, on a phone, half-distracted.** They are not studying the post. They
  are deciding whether it is worth studying.
- **They give the first line about one second.** Everything after it is earned.
- **They want one of three things**: to learn something they can use, to see a stake they
  recognise, or to disagree. A post that offers none of the three is wallpaper.
- **They are a person, not an audience.** Write to one reader. "You" is singular.

## Output structure

Every piece follows this shape, long or short.

1. **Hook** — stance or stake in the first line. One sentence, no preamble, no context-setting.
2. **Body** — the story or the insight, in the order it happened or the order it convinces.
3. **Close** — a question or an invitation to respond. One, and a real one.

**A thread repeats the shape at two scales.** The whole thread has a hook, a body, and a close;
each post in it opens with its own smallest hook so a reader who lands mid-thread keeps going.

## Rules

### Stance and voice

- **Lead with a point of view**, because a post with no position gives the reader nothing to agree
  or disagree with, and agreement is what makes them stop.
- **Use self-mention — "I", "we", "my"** — because a named first person turns a claim into
  testimony, which is harder to scroll past than a fact.
- **Put the cost in**, because a lesson with a price attached reads as true: what it cost you in
  days, money, or a mistake you had to admit.
- **Keep your own cadence**, because a post flattened into house style reads as a brand and
  readers discount brands.
- **One idea per post**, because a reader who has to hold two ideas holds neither.

### Reader engagement

- **Address the reader directly**, because "you" makes the post about them rather than about you.
- **Ask one question, at the end**, because a question in the middle stops the read and a second
  question splits the answer.
- **Name the reader's situation before you name your answer**, because recognition is what buys
  the second line.
- **Invite a specific response**, because "thoughts?" asks the reader to do the work of deciding
  what to say.
- **Make the first line survive alone**, because the platform truncates and the first line is
  often all a reader is shown.

### Hedging and boosting

- **Boost what you have evidence for and hedge what you do not**, because a post that hedges
  everything says nothing and a post that hedges nothing is a post the reader stops trusting.
- **Attach the number, the version, or the timebox** to every strong claim, because a specific
  claim can be checked and a vague one is read as a slogan.
- **Say "in my case" rather than "always"** when the evidence is one project, because an
  overclaim invites the one reply that discredits the whole post.
- **Never hedge the hook.** A hedged first line has no stake, and the stake is the hook.
- **Drop the credential-stacking**, because authority claimed in the post is authority the reader
  did not grant.

### Signposting

- **Signal the payoff in the first line** — the thread marker, "here is what I would do instead",
  the number of lessons — because a reader who knows what is coming reads on.
- **Number the parts when there is more than one**, because a numbered list tells the reader how
  much is left.
- **Give each line its own line.** White space is the only formatting the feed respects, and a
  wall of text is a scroll.
- **Keep transitions out.** "Furthermore", "in conclusion", and "that said" are document words
  that cost a line and add nothing in a feed.
- **End where the post ends.** No summary of what the reader just read.

## Avoid

- **A context-setting first line.** "Recently I have been thinking about…" spends the one second
  you get on nothing.
- **Broetry** — one sentence per line for its own sake, with a fake rhythm and no content.
  The line break is for reading, not for drama.
- **The engagement-bait close.** "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Comment YES below" asks for a reaction
  rather than a reply.
- **The humble-brag frame.** "I am so humbled to announce" reads as a brag with a disclaimer.
- **The false reveal.** "It is not about the code, it is about the people" — a reversal with no
  substance under it.
- **The list of negations.** "No fluff, no filler, no excuses" fills the space it claims to save.
- **A trailing comma clause** that adds a second thought the sentence did not need.
- **Hashtag stacks and borrowed urgency.** Three hashtags at most, and only ones a reader searches.
- **Claiming a result you cannot show.** A number you did not measure is the one thing the replies
  will find.
- **Recycling someone else's post as your own.** Credit the source in the post, not in a reply.

## Examples

**Weak:** "Here are some thoughts on database indexing."
**Strong:** "I wasted 3 days on a slow query. The fix was one line 🧵"
**Why:** shows a stake, uses self-mention, promises a payoff.

**Weak:** "Excited to share that we have shipped our new onboarding flow! Huge thanks to the whole
team for their hard work on this journey."
**Strong:** "We cut signup from 11 steps to 3. Conversion went up 40%. Here is the step we were
most afraid to remove."
**Why:** the claim carries numbers, the close points at the one thing a reader wants, and nobody
is thanked at the reader's expense.

**Weak:** "In my humble opinion, it could perhaps be argued that TypeScript might offer some
benefits for larger teams."
**Strong:** "TypeScript paid for itself on our team at about 40,000 lines. Below that I would not
bother."
**Why:** it takes a position, bounds it with a number, and hedges the scope instead of the claim.

## Final check

- Does the first line give a reason to keep reading, on its own, with everything after it hidden?
- Is there one idea, one stance, and one ask?
- Is every bold claim backed by something specific — a number, a version, a timebox?
- Is the hedging on the scope rather than on the hook?
- Does the close invite a reply a real person would type?
- Would the post survive the reader seeing only its first line and its last?
- Is there anything in it from the Avoid list?
